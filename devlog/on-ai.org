   There's large amounts of corruption and big tech just happened to be the largest player going into this

The only way to get a return on the mountains of money invested is to disrupt all labor.

* Regarding Agentic workflows or the insistence that we're at a higher abstraction layer

It's important not to make a categorical error about AI (which seems to be the dominant mistake amongst techy AI boosters).
It's not an abstraction layer.
An abstraction layer (i.e. machine code to assembly or assembly to high level language) is a
deterministic translation.
Sampling tokens based on hyperspace probability distributions is a stochastic process (not deterministic).

Agents are thus, at a fundamental level, chains of discrete probabilistic decisions.
Imagine a task broken down into 20 discrete steps,
Even if an individual agentic step has an extraordinarily high success rate of 95%
the probability that the entire 20-step workflow executes without a single error is calculated
as $0.95^{20}$.
That overall success rate plummets to ~36%.
So, more than 6 out of 10 runs will fail purely
due to the compounding math of sequential probabilities.

Unlike a deterministic abstraction layer where a bad instruction halts execution immediately
(e.g., a syntax error that stops a compiler), a probabilistic agent handles errors improvisationally which
exponentially compounds and causes "semantic drift"
This can lead to exponential spiraling if it hits a hard barrier (testing barrier) and break
invariant code elsewhere in codebase to self-correct, causing development thrashing and a proper mess.

I think we're touching an irreducible fault line here.
Code generation (or anything generation) is stochastic and is fundamentally unreliable,
thus guardrails (harnesses) are made that deterministically stear the generation,
but if those gaurdrails themselves are generated by the same stochastic processes they are meant to
police, they share the exact same blind spots, latent biases, and hallucination vectors
as the code they are checking (thus there has to be a prime mover / axiom i.e. a human that at least
kind of understands what the hell's going on, something that is able to jump out of a formal system)

[[https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=daWdQtSxgKo][More Mark Jivko on the topic]]
This kind of touches some deep philosophical ground (Godel's incompletes theorem & Baudrillardian hyperreality)

A more valid approach I've seen brough up recently from AI boosters is an anology to evolution, that evolution is a random (stochastic)
process and that if it works it works (just let the generative process explore a design or utility space until it does
what I want it to do)
I can kind of buy into this at first, but it also seems a lot weaker when you consider the driver
of evolution in the real world (biological or otherwise) is the immutable laws of physics.
I don't think there is a corresponding evolutionary pressure in software.
A piece of software can be completely broken, logically incoherent,
and full of security backdoors, yet still compile, pass a poorly written test suite, and run happily for months until a specific sequence of user inputs triggers a catastrophe

Without explanatory understanding, the system accumulates accidental complexity at an exponential rate and
is fragile to future incongruous interaction (something always comes up and without understanding the whole
it can never be sucessfully subsumed).
[[https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NM-zWTU7X-k][There is a differnce between knowing something and understanding it]]
You cannot outsource understanding; there is no such thing as passive learning.
